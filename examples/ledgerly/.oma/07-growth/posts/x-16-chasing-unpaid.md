# X — day 21 — chasing an unpaid invoice

**This is the pillar people cut, and it's the one that makes the account worth
following.** Rule for this row: no product name, no link, no "and that's why I
built…". It either stands as advice or it doesn't go out.

The schedule below is a plausible shape, not your experience. Replace it with
what you actually do — and if you don't have a practice, decide on one this week
and post it after you've run it once, not before.

## Post 1/4

```
Chasing a late invoice is easier if you decide the schedule before you need it.
Deciding in the moment means deciding while annoyed, which is how a reasonable
follow-up turns into a relationship you now have to repair.
```

## Post 2/4

```
Due date + 1: a one-line note that asks for nothing.

"INV-0042 went out on the 3rd and was due yesterday — anything you need from me
to get it processed?"

Assume the accounts payable process, not bad faith. It's usually the process.
```

## Post 3/4

```
+7 days: reply in the same email thread rather than starting a new one.

Same thread means the original invoice is right there, nobody has to search, and
it's noticeably harder to lose. New threads are how a chase becomes three
separate half-conversations.
```

## Post 4/4

```
+14 days: change the recipient, not the tone.

Whoever hired you is rarely whoever pays the bills. Asking your contact for the
right person in accounts is a normal question with a normal answer, and it beats
sending a fourth firmer email to someone who can't pay you anyway.
```
