# LinkedIn — day 10

## Post

```
I could always tell you what I was owed. I couldn't tell you when I last checked
that it was true.

Those feel like the same sentence and they are not, and the gap between them is
where freelance money goes missing.

A tracking spreadsheet drifts in a specific way. Nothing dramatic happens — a
payment lands while you're mid-project and you mean to update the row that
evening. A client pays two invoices in one transfer and you mark one. You
duplicate last month's tab to save time and inherit a status from last month
along with it. Every one of those is a thirty-second mistake, and none of them
announces itself. The file still looks authoritative. It's just describing a
week that has since passed.

The failure mode isn't that you lose money. It's that you stop being able to
answer "who owes me" without an audit, so you stop asking, so the answer gets
worse.

Two things helped me more than any tool:

Reconcile on a fixed day, not when you happen to think of it. Whatever the
system is, once a week you sit down and compare it against what actually landed
in the bank. Thinking of it is not a schedule.

Keep the number of places the status lives at exactly one. If paid/unpaid is
recorded in a spreadsheet AND in your email folders AND in your head, you don't
have a record, you have three opinions.

The tool I ended up building is downstream of both of those. But if you never
open the spreadsheet again after today and just do the weekly check, you'll be
ahead of where I was.
```
